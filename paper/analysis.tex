\section{What the Heavy Models Buy}
\label{sec:diagnosis}
Having mapped \emph{when} heavy models are worth their cost (\S\ref{sec:costbenefit}), we isolate \emph{what}
they irreducibly provide. On POG, the category ontology has a hard HR@1 ceiling of \textbf{37.6\%}, proven
across three formulations: in $62\%$ of cases a random negative sits in a \emph{more} complementary category
than the positive's true category, so no category-level signal can rank the positive first more often. The
7B exceeds this ceiling ($0.413$) by reasoning at the \emph{item} level. We tried five cheap ways to recover
the residual---larger frozen embeddings, a bge-large encoder, a hard-negative two-tower, a cross-attention
set-reasoner trained from scratch---and none close the last ${\sim}3.4$ points: it is not a representation
problem (bigger embeddings do not help) and not an architecture problem (the right architecture cannot be
learned from $14$K bundles). The irreducible edge is \textbf{pretrained combinatorial set-reasoning}---judging
whether \emph{this specific set} coheres.

\paragraph{Where that edge lives.} POG test positives are ${\sim}98\%$ non-co-occurring and $60\%$ cold, so
collaborative memorisation is near-useless on almost the entire test set and the aggregate score \emph{is},
in effect, the cold-item score. The 7B's edge over us ($0.413$ vs.\ $0.385$) is therefore spent exactly
where collaborative signal is absent and only item-level reasoning helps---the cold/complementarity
regime---rather than on warm, memorisable pairs. On the collaborative datasets the reverse holds:
co-occurrence saturates the warm bucket and the heavy models' margin shrinks to within $2$--$3\%$. This is
consistent with the stratified view in Fig.~\ref{fig:strat}, where every cheap collaborative signal is
exactly $0$ on cold items.

\paragraph{Two methodological cautions.} (i) The multiple-choice metric is misleading: a coarse category
ontology scores MC-HR@1 $\approx 0.32$ but full-ranking R@20 $\approx 0.001$---it only has to beat nine
random negatives, not retrieve. (ii) Reusing precomputed collaborative features (e.g.\ LightGCN) under a
re-split leaks co-occurrence of held-out items; in one early run this fabricated a $16\times$ ``win'' on POG.
Rebuilding split-dependent signals from the train fold removes it. Both cautions matter for anyone
benchmarking bundle construction.
